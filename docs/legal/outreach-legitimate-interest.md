# Outreach contacts: legitimate interest assessment

- **Status:** Draft by Claude (2026-10-17) for Dani to approve, amend or reject on the association's behalf. Not
  legal advice. Once approved, [personal-data §8a](../spec/personal-data.md#8a-outreach-contacts-m3) cites it as
  the basis.
- **Relates to:** [personal data](../spec/personal-data.md), [outreach](../spec/outreach.md), PLAN M5 step 6.

GDPR applies to what the association does with personal data, whatever the tool: a shared document, a spreadsheet
or this app. This assessment covers outreach records wherever they are kept, not only the ones in the backoffice.

## What is processed

- **Whose:** people who aren't members: politicians, journalists, academics, people at organisations.
- **What:** name, role, organisation, territory, professional contact details (a work address, the organisation's
  inbox, a work phone), the public source each came from and why they matter, tier, tags, the relationship stage,
  campaign targets and their status, and a timeline of notes. Notes may quote or summarise conversations held with
  them in their professional role, may be dated when the conversation happened, and may be private to the team
  member who wrote them (step 6).
- **From where:** public sources (institutional, party, media and university pages), the person themselves
  (replies, meetings), and introductions by members.
- **Not:** private life, home addresses, special categories (art. 9). A politician's positions stated in their
  public role are made public by them (art. 9(2)(e)); notes keep to positions on AI policy relevant to the
  advocacy, never personal beliefs, health or the like, as the app's hints already ask of notes.

## 1. Purpose

The association's advocacy: informing decision-makers and journalists about the risks of AI, coordinating who
contacts whom so nobody is written to twice or against their wishes, remembering what was said and agreed, and
honouring objections. A real, present and lawful interest, of the kind recital 47 recognises.

## 2. Necessity

The purpose can't be met without the data: writing to someone needs a professional address, coordinating a team
needs a shared record, and keeping commitments needs notes. What is kept is the minimum for that: professional
details only, off every list (only the contact's own page shows them); no gender inferred from a name; no
profiling, no automated decisions, no sending by the app; sources limited to public professional ones.

For contact details and role alone, Spain's LOPDGDD art. 19 presumes legitimate interest when they are only what's
needed to reach someone professionally, to deal with the body they work for. **Cited from memory: check the
wording in the BOE before relying on it.** Notes go beyond that presumption, hence this assessment.

## 3. Balancing

- **Expectations:** people in public office, journalists and academics reasonably expect civil-society groups to
  write to them in their professional role and to keep a record of the exchange.
- **Nature:** professional and mostly public. Notes on conversations are the most sensitive part, so they get the
  most safeguards.
- **Impact:** low. A letter they can ignore or answer; no decision is made about them; nothing is shared outside
  the association or sold.
- **Safeguards:** only outreach staff, with mandatory two-factor sign-in, see these records (ADR-0002); a private
  note is seen by its author alone; contact details stay off lists and out of the audit log; every change is
  audited; erasure on request, after which they are held so no queue offers them again (an art. 21 objection ends
  there); data on the association's own servers in the EU; no data sent to an AI provider (D10); nothing sends on
  its own.

**Conclusion:** the association's legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f)) is a valid basis, provided the safeguards
above hold and the people concerned are told, as below.

## 4. Telling them (art. 14)

Their data isn't obtained from them, so art. 14 applies: they must be told at the latest in the first
communication (art. 14(3)(b)). Since every contact is written to anyway, that is where: one line in the first
letter, linking the association's privacy notice, which gains an outreach paragraph. The privacy notice must also
name the controller and how to reach them (art. 14(1)(a)).

### Paragraph for the association's privacy notice

> **Contactos de incidencia.** Para nuestra labor de incidencia tratamos datos profesionales de personas con cargo
> público, periodistas, investigadores y personas de organizaciones: nombre, cargo, organización, datos de
> contacto profesionales, la fuente pública de la que los obtenemos y notas sobre nuestras conversaciones con
> ellas en su función profesional. Lo hacemos por nuestro interés legítimo en informar sobre los riesgos de la
> inteligencia artificial y en coordinar nuestro contacto (art. 6.1.f del RGPD). Los obtenemos de fuentes públicas
> (webs institucionales, de partidos, de medios o de universidades), de la propia persona o de quien nos la
> presenta. Solo accede nuestro equipo de relaciones externas; no los cedemos a nadie ni los usamos para enviar
> nada de forma automática. Los conservamos mientras sean útiles para esa labor o hasta que te opongas. Puedes
> acceder a ellos, rectificarlos, oponerte a su tratamiento o pedir que los borremos escribiendo a [correo de
> privacidad]; si te opones, dejamos de contactarte. También puedes reclamar ante la Agencia Española de
> Protección de Datos (aepd.es).

### Line for the first letter

> PauseAI España guarda tus datos profesionales y un registro de nuestro contacto para coordinar su labor de
> incidencia. Puedes ver cómo, oponerte o pedir que los borremos en [enlace a la política de privacidad].

Campaign templates are written in the app, so the team adds this line to each campaign's first letter.
